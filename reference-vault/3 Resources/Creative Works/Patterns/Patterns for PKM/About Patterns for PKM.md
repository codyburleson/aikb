# About Patterns for PKM

![[about-patterns-for-pkm-banner-img.webp]]
*AI-generated art ([Midjourney](https://www.midjourney.com/home))*

Back in 1977, an architect named [Christopher Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Alexander) dropped a book that would blow everyone’s minds — though maybe not in the way he expected. “[A Pattern Language: Towns, Buildings, Construction](https://www.amazon.com/Pattern-Language-Buildings-Construction-Environmental/dp/0195019199/ref=sr_1_1)” wasn’t just another dry architecture text. It was Alexander looking around and basically saying, “Hey, notice how humans keep solving the same problems in similar ways? Maybe we should write that down!”

![[a-pattern-language-book-cover.webp]]

He called these recurring solutions “patterns,” and here’s the cool part: he didn’t just list them randomly. He connected them like pieces of a giant puzzle, where each piece could be part of many different pictures. This wasn’t just a collection — it was a language for talking about good design.

Now, you might be wondering what architecture has to do with managing your digital notes and knowledge. Hold that thought, because there’s a plot twist coming. See, in the late 1980s, some software engineers got their hands on Alexander’s book and had what I can only imagine was a collective “Aha!” moment. They realized that programmers were also solving the same problems over and over again. Next thing you know, the “Gang of Four” published their own [pattern language for software design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_Patterns), and the rest is history.

![[design-patterns-book-cover.webp]]
## Why PKM Needs Its Own Pattern Language

Let’s be honest — we’re all drowning in information. Our digital lives are a mess of notes, bookmarks, random thoughts, and that article we swear we’ll read someday. But here’s the thing: we’re all facing surprisingly similar challenges. Whether you’re a researcher, a writer, a student, or just someone trying to keep their digital life organized, certain problems keep popping up.

That’s why we started collecting PKM patterns. And let me tell you, once you start looking, you see them everywhere. Here’s what we’ve spotted so far…

## Join the Pattern Hunt!

This list? It’s just a first pass, like a rough map of a territory we’re all exploring together. We probably missed some patterns that are obvious to you, or maybe you’ve developed your own solutions that could become patterns.

So, let’s make this interesting:

- Got a pattern we missed? Tell us about it!
- Could these patterns be organized differently? We're all ears.
- Found a clever way to combine some of these patterns? Share your secrets!
- Just want to tell us we’re completely wrong about something? Hey, that’s what comments are for!

Please think of this as the start of a conversation about how we can all get better at managing our personal knowledge. After all, if architects can have a pattern language for buildings, and programmers can have one for software, why shouldn’t we have one for organizing our digital thoughts?
